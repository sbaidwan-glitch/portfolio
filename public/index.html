Spring 2026 · Experimental Engineering Coursework · Team

Basilisk — Autonomous Surface Vehicle

A sensor-laden floating robot that measures wind and sun offshore to help site marine renewable energy.

Offshore wind and floating solar farms need good local data on wind speed, wind direction, and solar irradiance. For E80 we built Basilisk, an autonomous surface vehicle that logs all three while underway. I designed the multi-sensor instrumentation — a Hall-effect anemometer, a magnetic weathervane, and a photodiode transimpedance amplifier for irradiance — and integrated them with a Teensy 4.0 and microSD logging, calibrating each sensor against reference instruments before field deployment.

What we found Deployed at Dana Point, the vehicle showed no significant change in wind or irradiance with distance from shore. Solar power density was roughly 76× wind power density in the cove. The anemometer lost pulse accuracy at higher wind speeds, and the bot drifted off heading without closed-loop control — both clear next steps.

Team: Samara Baidwan, Ava Barasch, Andy Maldonado, Felix Peng

Fall 2025 · Intro to Engineering Design Coursework · Team

Lab Helper — Microcentrifuge Tube Capper

A bench tool that caps a rack of tubes 4.7× faster than by hand.

Our client, a biology lab technician, spent a chunk of every week snapping lids onto microcentrifuge tubes by hand — slow, and hard on the wrists. For E4 we designed a hinged press that closes a full row at once, paired with a 3D-printed rack that holds the tubes steady and survives ethanol sterilization. Everything had to cost under $30 and not damage the tubes or the user’s gloves.

Result 80 tubes capped in 51 seconds versus about four minutes by hand — 4.7× faster — for under $30.

Team: Samara Baidwan, Sophie, Luna, Soraya, Lucas

  • 3D printing
  • Laser cutting
  • Rapid prototyping
  • Design review
Summer 2026 · UNSW Sydney Research

Carbon-Bronze Alloy Development & Characterization

Developing and characterizing 21 experimental copper-based alloys for bushings and bearings.

Summer research at the University of New South Wales. I calculated target compositions, prepared alloy charges, and carried out arc melting, heat treatment, metallographic preparation, optical microscopy, and hardness testing across 21 experimental carbon-bronze alloys. I also improved an existing MATLAB image-analysis pipeline to quantify carbon area fraction and particle-size distributions, and fabricated Zr-based alloys for space nuclear-reactor cladding research.
