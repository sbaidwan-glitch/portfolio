Mechanical co-president building MuddSub’s next competition submarine.

Summer 2026 · UNSW Sydney Research

Carbon-Bronze Alloy Development & Characterization

Designing lead-free copper alloys that hold onto their carbon — 21 compositions, made and measured.

Lead makes bronze easy to machine and gives it the lubricity that sliding parts need, but it’s being legislated out of bushings and bearings on health and environmental grounds. Carbon is the obvious substitute — except it barely dissolves in copper, so the real problem is finding compositions and heat treatments that keep graphite in the microstructure. Over a summer at UNSW we made and characterized 21 experimental carbon-bronze alloys, using SEM and EDS to confirm the black inclusions were lubricating graphite rather than hard, abrasive carbides, and hardness and pin-on-disk friction testing to see how they behaved as they wore.

What we found Several of the 21 alloys retained significant carbon. Like a graphite pencil, they leave a carbon trail when smeared across paper — a promising sign they’ll hold the self-lubricating properties that bushings and bearings depend on.

With Katie Cheng and Joshua Tanksley · Advisors: Prof. Lori Bassman (HMC) and Prof. Kevin Laws (UNSW Sydney)

Spring 2026 · Experimental Engineering Coursework · Team

Basilisk — Autonomous Surface Vehicle

A sensor-laden floating robot that measures wind and sun offshore to help site marine renewable energy.

Offshore wind and floating solar farms need good local data on wind speed, wind direction, and solar irradiance. For E80 we built Basilisk, an autonomous surface vehicle that logs all three while underway. I designed two of the three multi-sensor instrumentations, the Hall-effect anemometer and magnetic weathervane, and integrated them with a Teensy 4.0 and microSD logging, calibrating each sensor against reference instruments before field deployment.

What we found Deployed at Dana Point, the vehicle showed no significant change in wind or irradiance with distance from shore. Solar power density was roughly 76× wind power density in the cove. The anemometer lost pulse accuracy at higher wind speeds, and the bot drifted off heading without closed-loop control — both clear next steps.

Team: Samara Baidwan, Ava Barasch, Andy Maldonado, Felix Peng

Fall 2025 · Intro to Engineering Design Coursework · Team

Lab Helper — Microcentrifuge Tube Capper

A bench tool that caps a rack of tubes 4.7× faster than by hand.

Our client, a biology lab technician, spent a chunk of every week snapping lids onto microcentrifuge tubes by hand — slow, and hard on the wrists. For E4 we designed a hinged press that closes a full row at once, paired with a 3D-printed rack that holds the tubes steady and survives ethanol sterilization. Everything had to cost under $30 and not damage the tubes or the user’s gloves.

Result 80 tubes capped in 51 seconds versus about four minutes by hand — 4.7× faster — for under $30.

Team: Samara Baidwan, Sophie, Luna, Soraya, Lucas
